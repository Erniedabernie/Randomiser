Rings, no monogram

Taking the E out leaves a hole where it sat, so here are six ways to close it. d is on the phone now — say a letter to swap. Judge them at 32 and 16 rather than 180; that is where an icon lives or dies.

a · As-was

The rings exactly as they were, with the mark simply lifted out. The literal answer — and the weakest at 16px, because an empty centre has nothing left to read.

643216

b · Filled centre

An Ocean disc where the mark was. Reads as a fingertip resting on glass, which is what the app actually shows.

643216

c · Four rings

A tighter ripple with no centre at all. Best sense of movement outward; muddles below 64px.

643216

d · Ink contact point Shipping

The same three rings with a small ink disc at the centre. Highest contrast of the six and the clearest at 16px — an unmistakable dot inside a target.

643216

e · Inner ring picked

The innermost ring in ink rather than Ocean: the app’s own "this one was picked" state, rendered as the icon.

643216

f · Bold

Two heavy rings and a disc. Loudest on a busy wallpaper, slightly blunt at 180px.

Worth knowing

  • This icon carries no monogram at all. BRAND.md treats the scalpel-E as the thing that makes an app icon yours; without it, the only thing tying this to the family is the Prussian #003366 tile. That is a real trade rather than a technicality — flagging it once, then leaving it with you.
  • Android crops maskable icons to a circle, so every maskable build scales the composition to 78% rather than trusting the inner-80% square.
  • Everything drawn earlier is still on disk in icons/ — ten question marks with the E as the dot, nine other randomiser metaphors, six dice faces. node make-icons.mjs <name> swaps any of them in.
